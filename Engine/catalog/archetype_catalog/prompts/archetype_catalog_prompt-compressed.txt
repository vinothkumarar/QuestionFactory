==================================================
1. QUESTION FACTORY HIERARCHY
==================================================

Concept
    ↓
Question Possibility
    ↓
Archetype
    ↓
Question

Concept defines knowledge.

Question Possibility defines the assessment objective.

Archetype defines HOW that Question Possibility is
assessed.

Question is the concrete instance.

Do NOT collapse these layers.

An Archetype is an assessment pattern and reasoning
pathway that can be reused to generate multiple
different questions from the SAME Question Possibility.

An Archetype is NOT:

• A Concept
• A Question Possibility
• A Topic
• A Learning Outcome
• A Property
• A Fact
• A Formula
• A Knowledge Point

==================================================
2. STRICT QUESTION POSSIBILITY BOUNDARY
==================================================

Every Archetype MUST assess the supplied Question
Possibility.

Do NOT:

• create Concept variants
• create Question Possibility variants
• introduce neighboring concepts
• introduce adjacent chapters
• introduce unrelated knowledge
• change the underlying assessment objective
• expand beyond the supplied Question Possibility

If a candidate primarily belongs to another Question
Possibility, reject it.

The supplied Question Possibility is the boundary.

==================================================
4. DISTINCTNESS AND MERGING
==================================================

The objective is:

COMPLETE DISTINCT ARCHETYPE SPACE

NOT maximum count.

NOT minimum count.

Merge two candidates only when their overall assessment
strategy and reasoning pathway are materially equivalent.

Evaluate:

• Direction of reasoning
• Information given
• Information inferred
• Decision process
• Constraint structure
• Reasoning stages
• Comparison method
• Validation method
• Deduction method
• Reverse inference
• Case analysis
• Required conclusion

Sharing the same formula, theorem, property, calculation,
or intermediate operation does NOT automatically make two
Archetypes identical.

The following MAY be distinct when academically valid:

• Forward reasoning vs reverse reasoning
• Ranking vs feasibility
• Prediction vs reconstruction
• Direct inference vs consistency validation
• Existence vs uniqueness
• Normal case vs exception case
• Single-condition vs multi-condition reasoning
• Direct determination vs constraint-based determination

Do NOT merge merely because candidates share an
intermediate operation.

Merge when a student would use essentially the same
overall assessment strategy from the supplied information
to reach the required conclusion.

IMPORTANT COVERAGE CHECK

Before merging candidates, verify whether they differ
in the TYPE OF EVIDENCE used to reach the conclusion.

The following may be distinct when independently valid:

• Structural evidence
• Numerical/equational evidence
• Periodic-trend evidence
• Electron-configuration evidence
• Stoichiometric evidence
• Charge-balance evidence
• Experimental/property evidence
• Reverse reconstruction
• Error/consistency validation

Do not merge these merely because they ultimately
support the same conclusion.

Merge only when the evidence structure and overall
assessment strategy are materially equivalent.

• Diagram vs text when reasoning is identical
• Data layout changes
• Presentation-only changes

Prefer one reusable Archetype over superficial variants.
