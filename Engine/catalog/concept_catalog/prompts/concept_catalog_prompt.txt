CONCEPT DEFINITION

A Concept is a knowledge domain.

A Concept represents WHAT a student must know.

A Concept is NOT:

• A question type
• A problem type
• A technique
• A trick
• A shortcut
• A method
• A question possibility
• An archetype

BAD EXAMPLES

A Concept must be:

• JEE relevant
• Examination worthy

DISCOVERY RULES

1. Discover ALL concepts.

2. Include:

   • Fundamental concepts
   • Intermediate concepts
   • Advanced concepts
   • JEE Main concepts
   • JEE Advanced concepts
   • Analytical concepts
   • Applied concepts

3. Do NOT merge unrelated concepts.

4. Do NOT create duplicate concepts.

5. Do NOT stop at common concepts.

6. Exhaustively enumerate the academic space.

7. If a topic begins with words such as:

   Find
   Determine
   Evaluate
   Count
   Compute
   Identify
   Prove

   then it is probably a Question Possibility,
   not a Concept.

   Do NOT include it.

CONCEPT BOUNDARY RULES

8. Generate Concepts ONLY for the supplied
   Subtopic.

9. A Concept must be directly teachable as
   part of the supplied Subtopic itself.

10. Do NOT expand into neighboring concepts,
    supporting theories, derived theories,
    specialized models, or later syllabus
    concepts.

11. If a Concept can reasonably exist as an
    independent Concept under a different
    Subtopic, do NOT include it.

12. Prefer core Concept decomposition over
    topic expansion.

13. Exhaustively enumerate the Concept Space
    of the supplied Subtopic, but remain
    strictly within Subtopic boundaries.

CONCEPT DEFINITION RULES

14. A Concept is a core knowledge idea,
    principle, property, process,
    entity, or relationship that can be
    taught independently within the
    supplied Subtopic.

15. Do NOT create separate Concepts for:

    • Named equations
    • Named constants
    • Named computational methods
    • Named mathematical models
    • Named derivation frameworks

16. Such items should be treated as tools
    used to analyze or calculate a Concept,
    not as Concepts themselves.

17. If multiple equations, constants,
    models, or methods exist to study the
    same underlying idea, create only the
    underlying Concept.

18. Prefer conceptual abstraction over
    mathematical implementation.
